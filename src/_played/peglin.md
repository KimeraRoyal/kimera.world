I've been playing indie roguelites in passing since I was a child, so I've been more or less aware of the general trends in the genre. Recently, the big successes everyone wants to emulate have been Balatro and Slay the Spire, mostly resulting in a string of titles whose elevator pitch is probably "it's like Balatro but, instead of being about poker, it's about betting on horse racing" or whatever.

Peglin marks exciting new territory because, instead of being Balatro but about a different type of gambling, it's Slay the Spire but about pachinko[^1]. That's slightly different!!! I thought it was incorporated decently, being well integrated into both combat and, charmingly, how you choose the path to take between levels - trying to land your ball in the specific hole corresponding to the room you want to move to.

Less thought seems to have been put into the framework that pachinko gimmick is built on, particularly with the lack of complexity ramping as the game fills the screen with meaningless and somewhat obtuse keywords as early as the first encounter. Outside of some genuinely lovely music, the game feels adequate but safe with its gameplay, setting, and art lacking any real identity, authorial voice, or otherwise standout characteristics.

The levels are nothing, the characters are all stock fantasy fodder, the map is literally exactly the same as Slay the Spire except for the cute gimmick of how you traverse it. What really sealed my philosophical issues with this game was that they spend time giving all of the keywords silly ball themed names but then refer to your collection of orbs as a "deck". God forbid you call it something on-theme like a pouch or whatever. It's a deck because it's called a deck in Slay the Spire[^2]. This game is fine but I *knew* it was going to be fine because I've played the better games that did it all first - recently[^3]!

A lack of identity isn't a unique issue to Peglin, instead feeling like a natural result of the "it's like X meets Y" method of game ideation. It's not impossible - Nubby's Number Factory is a game with a *very* similar concept to Peglin while actually having a personality - but it's certainly to be expected for most of these games. There's something to be said about the dangers of "safe" in that it can very easily become "derivative".

I'm sure there are many people who enjoy playing games like Peglin[^4] - there's a lot of genuine benefit to the kind of design that takes existing motifs and mechanics and deploys them in various combinations - but will any of them remember it in a decade the way they'll remember the games it's inspired by? Does a game *need* to stand out or try to be memorable? You can find out by playing my new roguelike, which is like if Art Philosophy Debates met Balatro.

[^1]: Peggle is obviously the main inspiration but they can't say that in the tagline.

[^2]: Your permanent passive upgrades are called relics, which is *slightly* less egregious.

[^3]: Slay the Spire is only 5 years older than Peglin!!!

[^4]: I don't just say this to couch my negative opinions about a video game during a time period where people are overly self conscious and feel obligated to defend every single thing they like from any negativity. It's important to remember that these things have an audience and those people have genuine reasons to enjoy the work.
